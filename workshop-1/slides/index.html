  4. Slide 4 text

    The basics

    A lab report is not an essay

    EssayLab report

    PurposeArgues a point of viewReports what was done and found

    StructureFlowing paragraphs, few headingsFixed sections with subheadings

    VoiceCan be personal (“I think…”)Impersonal and past tense

    EvidenceQuotes and examplesData in tables and figures

    OpinionThroughoutOnly in Discussion, with evidence

  5. Slide 5 text

    Structure

    IMRaD: four main sections

    Start with a Title that names the reactants and the type of reaction.

    IIntroduction

    Why it matters

    Includes

    Background and theoryBalanced equationAims

    MMethod

    What you did

    Includes

    Detail to repeat itWhy each step is used

    RResults

    What you found

    Includes

    Tables and figuresCalculationsPercentage yield

    DDiscussion

    What it means

    Includes

    Compare with sourcesEvaluationConclusion (if asked)

    End with References in Harvard (or RSC for chemists!) style. Some modules want the Conclusion as its own section: check your brief.

  6. Slide 6 text

    Method

    Describe it, then justify it

    DescribeEnough detail to repeat it: amounts, equipment, conditions, timings.

    JustifySay why each key step or piece of equipment is used.“Crystals were washed with chilled water so impurities dissolved but little product was lost.”

    Raw data goes in the Results, not the Method.

  7. Slide 7 text

    Presenting data

    Tables and figures

    Tables

    Number and title above

    Units in the column headings

    Always state your percentage yield

    Figures and graphs

    Number and caption below

    Label both axes, with units

    Mention every figure in the text

    Ask: does this need a graph at all?

  8. Slide 8 text

    Discussion

    Every claim needs evidence

    ComparePut your result beside a published value, and cite it.

    JudgeSay whether it’s good. For an organic synthesis, markers expect around 50% or more to count as a good yield.

    ExplainUse theory to explain differences, such as why impurities lower a melting point.

    Use journals, textbooks and data books, not general websites.

  9. Slide 9 text

    Evaluation

    Be specific

    Vague

    “Human error”“Unknown factors affected the results”“Be more careful next time”

    Specific

    Product was lost on the filter paper at each filtration stepThe sample was probably still wet when weighed, so its mass was too highAn acid chloride could replace the anhydride for a faster, more complete reaction

  10. Slide 10 text

    References

    Harvard referencing

    In the text…has a melting point of 114 °C (Author, Year).

    In the reference list

    Full details, in alphabetical order, with access dates for websites. Use academic sources where you can.

    For the full format, use the guide from your earlier report-writing workshop.

  16. Three problems:

    • It uses “I”.
    • A liquid is measured, not “weighed on a scale”.
    • It’s one long run-on sentence.

    Better: “Ethanoic anhydride (1.0 cm³) was added, and the mixture was stirred until it dissolved.”

    Slide 16 text

    Part 2 · Writing style

    Write it like a scientist

    Full sentences, accurate punctuation

    Paragraphs, not one long sentence

    Past tense for what you did

    No “I” or “we”

    Concise, academic tone

    Try it: spot the mistakes

    “…next I weighed 1ml of ethanoic anhydride on a scale, added it to the solution of phenyl ammonium chloride and stirred until dissolved.”

    How many problems can you find? Talk to your table.

    • 1. Results. It’s a value you worked out.
    • 2. Evaluation (Discussion is also fine). It explains a weakness.
    • 3. Introduction. It’s background.
    Slide 17 text

    Try it yourself

    Where does it go?

    Which section does each sentence belong in? Decide as a table.

    1. “The percentage yield was 3.83%.”

    2. “Some product was probably lost during filtration.”

    3. “Acetanilide was once used as a painkiller.”

    IntroductionMethodResultsDiscussionEvaluation

    • A. Vague. Which results, and why?
    • B. Specific. It names the cause and the effect.
    • C. Vague. Which errors?

    Better A: “The yield was low because product was lost on the filter paper.”

    Slide 18 text

    Try it yourself

    Vague or specific?

    Would a marker give credit for these? Decide as a table.

    A. “The results were not very accurate.”

    B. “Some crystals stayed in the conical flask after transfer, which lowered the yield.”

    C. “Errors may have occurred during the experiment.”

    Try making one vague point specific

    Questions and next steps

    Three things to take away

    Follow the structure: sections, past tense, no “I”.

    Present data properly: numbered tables, units, yields.

    Back every claim with evidence, and be specific.

    Task 2 answers · Part A · yours don’t need to match word for word

    Model rewrites

    [1] and [2]

    Phenyl ammonium chloride (1.0 g) was dissolved in water (15 cm³). Ethanoic anhydride (1.0 cm³) was added and stirred until dissolved.

    Sodium ethanoate (3.0 g) in water (10 cm³) was added, and the mixture was stirred for 10 minutes.

    [3] to [5]

    The crude product was filtered under reduced pressure and washed with ice-cold water.

    Crystals formed after about 5 minutes and were filtered and dried.

    The melting point was measured. (The values go in the Results.)

  22. Slide 22 text

    Task 2 answers · Part B

    What’s wrong with the table and graph?

    The table

    No number or title

    No units in the headings

    Masses and melting point mixed together

    No percentage yield

    The graph

    “Chart Title” and “Series1” left in

    No axis labels or units

    A line joins two separate values

    No numbered caption, never mentioned in the text

    Two numbers don’t need a graph at all
